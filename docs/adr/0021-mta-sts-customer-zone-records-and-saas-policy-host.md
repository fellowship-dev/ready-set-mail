# MTA-STS writes the customer's own records and is served from a Cloudflare for SaaS policy host

One path for hosted and self-host: the three MTA-STS records (`mta-sts.<d>` CNAME, `_smtp._tls.<d>` TXT, `_mta-sts.<d>` TXT) go into the customer's own zone, through their Cloudflare connection when one reaches the zone and by hand otherwise, and this Worker serves the policy on `mta-sts.<d>`. On the hosted service the certificate for that hostname comes from a Cloudflare for SaaS custom hostname in the operator's zone, with a Worker route to this Worker (`src/mta-sts/policy-host.ts`). TLS reports go to the shared address `tls-rpt@<INTAKE_DOMAIN>`, routed by the domain inside the report (extends [ADR 0006](0006-one-shared-hosted-report-address.md)). The hosted records no longer host MTA-STS.

Basis: Cloudflare cannot answer HTTPS for `mta-sts.<customer domain>` from the operator's account any other way (a Worker cannot present a certificate for a name it does not own; the getting-started page of Cloudflare for SaaS is the source), and Workers cannot open outbound connections on port 25, so the product cannot probe a customer's mail server: TLS reports from senders are the evidence (RFC 8461 section 5, RFC 8460).

**Decisions made by the implementer: confirm**
- **Refresh and automatic writes** (extends [ADR 0004](0004-dns-writes-need-an-explicit-apply.md), whose last bullet says a new automatic write needs a new ADR). The daily refresh runs in `runHostedCron` (11:00 UTC, once for the whole database) on the hosted service, and in the existing per-workspace 08:00 branch on self-host (skipped on the hosted service, so it never runs twice). It rewrites the `_mta-sts` id when the MX list changes, switches an enforcing domain back to testing when the list grew (new hosts have no TLS evidence), and deletes operator-side custom hostnames and Worker routes this product made that no enabled row owns. It also creates and deletes the operator's custom hostname and route when a person turns MTA-STS on or off. Basis: a changed MX list that is not published makes senders fail or skip TLS; the 08:00 job already did the first of these on self-host. Whether the hosted service runs this job at 11:00 is the open decision "Hosted cron jobs" in [OPEN.md](OPEN.md): this record does not close it.
- **max_age is 86400 in both modes** (read from the row, one call site, `policyText` in `src/mta-sts/serve.ts`). Basis: a short cache makes an MX change, a mistake and Disable take effect within a day; RFC 8461 section 3.2 says "weeks or greater" is typical, which is an expectation, not a requirement. Raising it for enforce (Proton's own policy uses 604800) is for the owner.
- **Disable is offered only in testing**, and only once the last switch from enforce to testing is older than the row's max_age (or the domain was never in enforce). From enforce the page says to switch to testing first. Switching to testing is always allowed and is RFC 8461 section 8.3's first step in effect (a mode that refuses nothing, with a new id; testing instead of none so TLS reports keep coming). There is no teardown queue. Basis: senders keep a policy up to max_age, and a removed policy while one is cached in enforce mode can fail mail.
- **Domain delete, eviction and a lapsed proof tear down at once**, best effort: the customer's records by id with the usual kept/why report (`removeMtaSts` in `src/domains/remove.ts`), the operator's custom hostname and route by stored id. What a failure leaves behind, the daily job removes (`reconcileOperatorZone` in `src/mta-sts/refresh.ts`): routes to this Worker named `mta-sts.*` with no enabled row, and the custom hostname of the same name.
- **Hosted records no longer host MTA-STS.** The `mta_sts` field of `POST /api/domains/:id/hosted` is answered 400; stored hosted rows keep their cleanup (`disableHosted`); the columns stay, unused. A domain with hosted records cannot turn MTA-STS on (its `_smtp._tls` is already a pointer), and a domain with MTA-STS cannot enable hosted records.
- **Notices are state on the config row** (`notice`, `notice_at`), shown on the MTA-STS page and the DNS tools card, dismissible by an owner or admin. There is no in-app notification system and no email for these.
- **The operator's own zone.** `strategyFor` in `src/mta-sts/policy-host.ts` has two ways: `worker_route` (a proxied CNAME to a target in the same account plus, hosted, a Worker route) for self-host and for the operator's own zone apex, and `saas_hostname` (a DNS-only CNAME, a custom hostname and a Worker route) for every other hosted domain. A custom hostname is never created inside `POLICY_HOST_ZONE`. A name below the operator's zone (`mail.<zone>`) has no way: it gets no certificate from Universal SSL (which covers only the apex and one label) and may not be a custom hostname either, so it is refused with a sentence.
- **Enforce needs the evidence** (`enforceReadiness` in `src/mta-sts/evidence.ts`): at least 7 days in testing (CHANGELOG 1.2.0 already promised it), a TLS report after the current policy id was published with successful MTA-STS sessions, none of the blocking failure types in the last 14 days, and every MX host a hostname matching the policy. Each TLS report stores its MTA-STS sessions apart (`sts_success`, `sts_failure`, `sts_failure_types`); the totals stay.
- **Hosted gate.** MTA-STS is available to every hosted workspace once the operator has set up `POLICY_HOST_ZONE`, `POLICY_HOST_TARGET` and `POLICY_HOST_TOKEN`, the workspace is admitted to TLS reports (`workspaceMayConnectReports`) and the instance holds fewer than `MAX_POLICY_HOSTS` (default 100, the hostnames Cloudflare's plans include; each further hostname costs the operator $0.10). Raising the ceiling and any price are for the owner (`mayUseMtaSts` in `src/hosted/plan-limits.ts`).
- Manual (no connection reaches the zone) is hosted only. Self-host without a connection that reaches the zone has no way to get a certificate for `mta-sts.<domain>` and is told so.

**Consequences**
- New automatic writes, in addition to the list in ADR 0004: the daily MTA-STS refresh (id and MX list, mode back to testing), and the operator-side custom hostname and Worker route (created on enable, deleted on disable, domain removal, eviction, a lapsed proof, or by the daily reconcile). They pass `operatorPolicy` in `src/mta-sts/policy-host.ts` (custom hostnames and Worker routes of one zone, never a DNS record) and the customer's records still pass `assertDnsWriteAllowed`.
- Migration `migrations/0020_mta_sts_hosted.sql` is additive (`packages/db/UPGRADING.md`).
- The hosted records' MTA-STS parts (the hosted writers for the MTA-STS record, its refresh job, the hosted-id policy host) are gone. This is a breaking change for anyone who sent `mta_sts: true` to the hosted route (CHANGELOG).
