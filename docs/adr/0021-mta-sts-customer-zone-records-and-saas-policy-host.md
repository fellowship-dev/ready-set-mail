# MTA-STS writes the customer's own records and is served from a Cloudflare for SaaS policy host

One path for hosted and self-host: the three MTA-STS records (`mta-sts.<d>` CNAME, `_smtp._tls.<d>` TXT, `_mta-sts.<d>` TXT) go into the customer's own zone, through their Cloudflare connection when one reaches the zone and by hand otherwise, and this Worker serves the policy on `mta-sts.<d>`. On the hosted service the certificate for that hostname comes from a Cloudflare for SaaS custom hostname in the operator's zone, and the Worker is reached through ONE route declared in the hosted deploy config (`src/mta-sts/policy-host.ts`, below). TLS reports go to the shared address `tls-rpt@<INTAKE_DOMAIN>`, routed by the domain inside the report (extends [ADR 0006](0006-one-shared-hosted-report-address.md)). The hosted records no longer host MTA-STS.

Basis: Cloudflare cannot answer HTTPS for `mta-sts.<customer domain>` from the operator's account any other way (a Worker cannot present a certificate for a name it does not own; the getting-started page of Cloudflare for SaaS is the source), and Workers cannot open outbound connections on port 25, so the product cannot probe a customer's mail server: TLS reports from senders are the evidence (RFC 8461 section 5, RFC 8460).

**Decisions made by the implementer: confirm**
- **Refresh and automatic writes** (extends [ADR 0004](0004-dns-writes-need-an-explicit-apply.md), whose last bullet says a new automatic write needs a new ADR). The daily refresh runs in `runHostedCron` (11:00 UTC, once for the whole database) on the hosted service, and in the existing per-workspace 08:00 branch on self-host (skipped on the hosted service, so it never runs twice). It first reconciles every enabled row (the custom hostname exists and is not moved, the declared route is in the zone, and a fetch of the policy URL proves it is served: next bullet), then rewrites the `_mta-sts` id when the MX list changes, switches an enforcing domain back to testing when the list grew (new hosts have no TLS evidence), finishes the policies being retired, and deletes operator-side custom hostnames that no row owns. It also creates and deletes the operator's custom hostname when a person turns MTA-STS on or off. Basis: a changed MX list that is not published makes senders fail or skip TLS; the 08:00 job already did the first of these on self-host. Whether the hosted service runs this job at 11:00 is the open decision "Hosted cron jobs" in [OPEN.md](OPEN.md): this record does not close it.
- **max_age follows the mode: 86400 in testing, 604800 in enforce** (`maxAgeFor` in `src/mta-sts/config.ts`, used by `policyText`). Basis: RFC 8461 section 3.2 expects "weeks or greater" (a short cache lets an attacker wait it out) and Proton serves 604800 in enforce; section 5 says to start in testing, where a mistake or an MX change should heal within a day. A domain that receives no TLS reports can never switch to enforce, and the page says so.
- **Disable is offered only in testing**, and only once the last switch from enforce to testing is older than the ENFORCE policy's max_age, a week (or the domain was never in enforce). From enforce the page says to switch to testing first. Switching to testing is always allowed and is RFC 8461 section 8.3's first step in effect (a mode that refuses nothing, with a new id; testing instead of none so TLS reports keep coming). There is no teardown queue. Basis: senders keep a policy up to max_age, and a removed policy while one is cached in enforce mode can fail mail.
- **Domain delete, eviction and a lapsed proof retire the policy, RFC 8461 section 8.3** (`src/mta-sts/retire.ts`; was: tear down at once, which leaves senders that cached an enforce policy refusing mail for up to a week). The policy is served as `mode: none` (max_age 86400) by name and without proof (it can only stop senders from refusing), a new `_mta-sts` id is published where a connection can write it, and the custom hostname, the records and the entry go when the last served policy's max_age has passed since the change (`retire_after`; a week after an enforce policy or one that left enforce within a week, a day otherwise). The entry is its own table, `mta_sts_retiring`, because a removed domain's config row is deleted with the domain. A policy nobody fetched goes at once. Turning MTA-STS on again takes the host back. The page and the removal answer say what senders may still do meanwhile. What a failure leaves behind, the daily job removes (`reconcileOperatorZone`): custom hostnames `mta-sts.*` with no row and no retiring entry, and per-domain routes of an earlier build.
- **A setup nobody finishes is stopped after 7 days** (Cloudflare deletes a custom hostname that was not validated within 7 days anyway): the hostname and records are deleted, the slot is free, the row stays off with one notice.
- **Hosted records no longer host MTA-STS.** The `mta_sts` field of `POST /api/domains/:id/hosted` is answered 400; stored hosted rows keep their cleanup (`disableHosted`); the columns stay, unused. A domain with hosted records cannot turn MTA-STS on (its `_smtp._tls` is already a pointer), and a domain with MTA-STS cannot enable hosted records.
- **Notices are state on the config row** (`notice`, `notice_at`), shown on the MTA-STS page and the DNS tools card, dismissible by an owner or admin. There is no in-app notification system and no email for these.
- **The operator's own zone.** `strategyFor` in `src/mta-sts/policy-host.ts` has two ways: `worker_route` (a proxied CNAME to a target in the same account) for self-host and for the operator's own zone apex, and `saas_hostname` (a DNS-only CNAME and a custom hostname) for every other hosted domain. A custom hostname is never created inside `POLICY_HOST_ZONE`. A name below the operator's zone (`mail.<zone>`) has no way: it gets no certificate from Universal SSL (which covers only the apex and one label) and may not be a custom hostname either, so it is refused with a sentence.
- **Enforce needs the evidence** (`enforceReadiness` in `src/mta-sts/evidence.ts`): at least 7 days in testing (CHANGELOG 1.2.0 already promised it), setup verified by the policy fetch, ONE counted TLS report (below) whose period ended after the current policy id was published with successful MTA-STS sessions, none of the blocking failure types in the counted reports of the last 14 days, and every MX host a hostname matching the policy. Each TLS report stores its MTA-STS sessions apart (`sts_success`, `sts_failure`, `sts_failure_types`); the totals stay.
- **TLS reports are authenticated before they count** (RFC 8460 section 3: "Reports sent via SMTP MUST contain a valid DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM) [RFC6376] signature by the reporting domain. Reports lacking such a signature MUST be ignored by the recipient."). The ONE rule DMARC reports use decides it (`carrierFromHeaders`, `guardEnvelope`, `decideTier` in `src/dmarc/reporter-auth.ts`, registry `src/dmarc/known-reporters.ts`): the receiver's own passing DKIM result, the From address and the report's `contact-info` must match the registry's TLS identity of Google or Microsoft (evidence: public fixtures of real report mail, cited in the registry), a DMARC pass that may rest on SPF is not enough, and the `TLS-Report-Submitter` header (section 5.3: "MUST match the value found in the domain of the "contact-info""), if present, and the contact-info domain must be the signing organisation. Anything else is stored with its tier (unknown or unauthenticated), under the uncounted budgets and retention, and is never evidence. The period must be one day plus an hour of skew (section 4.1), not in the future and not older than 14 days; a replay (same reporter, same report-id) changes nothing.
- **Evidence rule: ONE counted report is enough (to confirm).** Enforce needs at least one counted report, not two reporters. A counted report can only be forged by forging Google's or Microsoft's DKIM, so a second reporter adds coverage, not authenticity, and most small domains receive mail from only one of the two: requiring both would lock them out. The enforce gate always asks for counted reports, whatever `REPORT_COUNTING` says (it decides whether mail is refused, so a stranger must never move it); the numbers shown follow `REPORT_COUNTING` as for DMARC. On a self-hosted server that has not set `AUTHSERV_ID` and `AUTHSERV_HEADER` nothing can be authenticated, so enforce is not offered and the page says so.
- **The policy is proved by fetching it, and the id waits for the proof.** One reconcile (`src/mta-sts/reconcile.ts`) fetches `https://mta-sts.<d>/.well-known/mta-sts.txt` (no redirects, a short timeout, no cache): status 200, text/plain, the exact policy, over a valid certificate. The page's "served" is that result and nothing else, and the `_mta-sts` id is not published (setup, mode change, MX change) while it fails.
- **ONE declared route serves every policy host.** The hosted deploy config declares `{ "pattern": "*/.well-known/mta-sts.txt", "zone_name": "<POLICY_HOST_ZONE>" }`. Cloudflare (developers.cloudflare.com/workers/wrangler/deprecations/): "Cloudflare dashboard-defined routes will not be added alongside Wrangler-defined routes", and when both exist only the Wrangler-defined routes are valid; so a route made through the API per domain is lost at the next deploy of a config that declares routes; custom hostnames enter the operator's zone and "The `*/*` route pattern matches all traffic entering your zone" (cloudflare-for-platforms worker-as-origin). The reconcile only checks that the route exists and re-creates that exact pattern, with an error in the log, when a deploy lost it; after every deploy the operator triggers it (`POST /api/instance/mta-sts/reconcile`, instance owner) and reads the result. Not verified live: that the pattern is accepted for custom hostnames of the zone.
- **Policy hosts answer one path.** `run_worker_first` is on for every request, and the Worker answers a policy host before anything else; every other path, including the dashboard's files, is a 404.
- **Hosted gate.** MTA-STS is available to every hosted workspace once the operator has set up `POLICY_HOST_ZONE`, `POLICY_HOST_TARGET` and `POLICY_HOST_TOKEN`, the workspace is admitted to TLS reports (`workspaceMayConnectReports`) and the instance holds fewer than `MAX_POLICY_HOSTS` (default 100, the hostnames Cloudflare's plans include; each further hostname costs the operator $0.10). Raising the ceiling and any price are for the owner (`mayUseMtaSts` in `src/hosted/plan-limits.ts`).
- Manual (no connection reaches the zone) is hosted only. Self-host without a connection that reaches the zone has no way to get a certificate for `mta-sts.<domain>` and is told so.

**Consequences**
- New automatic writes, in addition to the list in ADR 0004: the daily MTA-STS refresh (id and MX list, mode back to testing), and the operator-side custom hostname (created on enable, refreshed or re-created by the reconcile, deleted on disable, when a retired policy has expired, when a setup was abandoned, or by the daily leftover cleanup) and, when a deploy lost it, the one declared route. They pass `operatorPolicy` in `src/mta-sts/policy-host.ts` (custom hostnames and Worker routes of one zone, never a DNS record) and the customer's records still pass `assertDnsWriteAllowed`.
- Migration `migrations/0020_mta_sts_hosted.sql` is additive (`packages/db/UPGRADING.md`).
- The hosted records' MTA-STS parts (the hosted writers for the MTA-STS record, its refresh job, the hosted-id policy host) are gone. This is a breaking change for anyone who sent `mta_sts: true` to the hosted route (CHANGELOG).
