# Open decisions

Not decided. Do not build as if they were; ask the owner, then record the answer as an ADR and delete the line here.

| Decision | Today on `main` | Options on the table |
|---|---|---|
| **Level ladder** (which rungs the product shows) | Two ladders: the dashboard's three rungs (Delivering / Authenticated / Protected) and the agent report's four (Unprotected / Monitoring / Enforced / Hardened). See `docs/single-sources.md`. | Dashboard's three rungs (coordinator's pick) or the agent's four. One ladder in `packages/dns-checks` either way. |
| **Sign-in code lifetime** | 10 minutes (`CODE_SECONDS` in `src/auth/codes.ts`). | Keep, or lengthen so the code outlives a slow inbox. |
| **Open hosted reports to every customer** | `HOSTED_CONNECT_REPORTS=owner` by default: only the operator's own workspaces may connect reports (`src/hosted/fixed-address.ts`). | `all`, after large-report handling, forged-report weighting and storage caps are judged ready (`docs/hosted.md`). |
| **Hosted cron jobs** | Four crons (`wrangler.jsonc`, `src/cron.ts`): three run once per workspace (08:00 daily, the Monday 09:00 digest, and 11:00, which also refreshes flattened SPF records: nothing on hosted unless `HOSTED_SPF_FLATTENING` admits a workspace, ADR 0027); 04:00 (data lifecycle) runs once; 11:00 also runs, on the hosted service, the hosted job once (`src/hosted/cron.ts`, including billing's reconcile, the lifecycle emails and, proposed in [ADR 0021](0021-mta-sts-customer-zone-records-and-saas-policy-host.md), the MTA-STS refresh). The older `0 10` trigger is answered by the same SPF refresh. | Which jobs the hosted service runs, and how often. Not decided: ADR 0021 proposes running the MTA-STS refresh in the 11:00 hosted job. |
| **Client access request** (an agency asks a client for DNS access) | Not built. | A request link the client opens without an account: connect Cloudflare to the agency's workspace, or get records to paste. |
| **SPF flattening price (O-13)** | A per-domain add-on (ADR 0028). No price: `capability` (`src/hosted/plan-limits.ts`) is on for self-host and, on hosted, only for workspaces `HOSTED_SPF_FLATTENING` admits. | A price with a basis, a subscription item per domain in the plan (#180), and what a lapse does: keep the last good record and stop refreshing, restore the original, or a grace period. |
| **Hosted plan pricing** | Plan caps are variables (`src/hosted/plan-limits.ts`); the only priced item is Fix one domain (`src/billing/offer.ts`). | Free tier and paid plans: not decided. |
| **Fix one domain scope wording** | `OFFER_SCOPE` in `src/billing/offer.ts` still says the job moves DMARC to reject, which contradicts ADR 0013. | The exact replacement sentence (owner to approve), then one change to `offer.ts` and every place that repeats it. |
| **DMARC discovery: tree walk and `psd=` (RFC 9989) instead of the Public Suffix List** | The PSL decides the organizational domain everywhere (`organizationalDomain` in `packages/dns-checks/src/org-domain.ts`; `lookupDmarc` asks the name, then its organizational domain). `psd=`, a record at a name above the organizational domain, and names of more than eight labels are not read. No major receiver has announced tree-walk support (Google and Microsoft still document RFC 7489; a third-party tracker lists all three majors as unknown, 2026-10-01), so the PSL answer matches what receivers do today. The answers differ only for a record with `psd=n` or `psd=y`, a PSL private-suffix operator that publishes a record without `psd=y`, a name with more than eight labels, and a suffix newer than the bundled `tldts` snapshot. The PSL also lists private suffixes (such as `*.vercel.app`) that the tree walk does not treat specially, which is another way the two disagree. | Keep the PSL until a major receiver reports the discovery method "treewalk" in its aggregate reports (RFC 9990 section 3.1.1.5; the parser does not keep that field yet: a one-line parse-and-count decides it), then implement the tree walk beside it, in one module. |
| **Multiple DMARC records** (RFC 9989 section 4.10: all of them are discarded during the tree walk) | `discoverDmarc` in `packages/dns-checks/src/dmarc.ts` returns status `multiple` at the first name (the name itself, then its organizational domain) that has more than one DMARC record, and does not look at the next name. The lookup and the report then say several records exist, so receivers ignore all of them and DMARC does not apply (`dmarc-problem.ts`, `report.ts`). | Whether, in a tree walk, discarding the records at one name means continuing to the next name up. Not decided; the PSL-based lookup stops at the name with several records. |
| **`t=y` test mode** (RFC 9989 section 4.7) | The policy answer does not read `t=`: `effectivePolicy` and `lookupDmarc` in `packages/dns-checks` ignore it, so a record with `t=y` is reported at its `p=`. Only the rollout reads it: `src/dmarc/rollout.ts` writes `t=y` steps (quarantine and reject with testing on), `src/api/router.ts` reads the live `t=` of the record for the rollout step (`current_testing`), and `src/analytics/readiness.ts` words the next step with it. | How the level ladder and the sentences about what receivers do treat a record with `t=y` (for example, as the next-weaker policy). One change in `packages/dns-checks`, then every sentence that states the policy in force. |
| **Tips on the hosted service** (`LIFECYCLE_EMAILS_ON=tips`) | Off. The instance sends none until `LIFECYCLE_EMAILS_ON` names `tips`, and then only to a person who turned them on in Settings (no default, for owners or admins). | Whether, and when, to turn tips on for the live instance. |
